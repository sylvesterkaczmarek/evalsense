<Typography variant="body2" gutterBottom>
  EvalSense's QAGS-derived metric measures agreement between answers to questions
  generated from the evaluated text and a comparison text. It is a configurable
  adaptation, not an implementation of the published QAGS protocol.
</Typography>

<Typography variant="body2" gutterBottom>
  <Link href="https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.450/">Published QAGS</Link>
  {" "}generates answer-conditioned questions from a summary and compares
  answers from that summary and its source. Its question filtering includes an
  answer-consistency check. EvalSense instead generates questions from both texts
  and combines them before QA. The validation reported in the original paper does
  not establish the validity of these variants.
</Typography>

<Typography variant="body2" gutterBottom>
  Questions must be on separate lines. List prefixes and surrounding whitespace are removed;
  malformed lines are filtered and text duplicates are counted once across both
  lists. This does not verify semantic relevance or factual grounding. Each retained
  question has equal weight. Raw question outputs, retained-question sources and
  filtering counts are recorded for review.
</Typography>

<Typography variant="subtitle1" gutterBottom>
  Evaluation Modes
</Typography>

<Typography variant="body2">
- **Ternary mode:** Answers must be complete yes/no/unknown labels. Accuracy is the fraction of equal labels, including explicit unknown/unknown pairs. Unknown agreement does not establish factual support. The existing Coverage and Groundedness outputs divide matching yes/yes pairs by reference yes answers and evaluated-text yes answers, respectively. These are question-dependent positive-answer ratios; negative-fact and question-polarity limitations remain.
- **Exact mode:** Nonempty answers are compared after trimming whitespace and ignoring case. This can penalise semantically equivalent wording.
- **Judge mode:** An LLM compares nonempty answers with a complete binary response. Scores average those comparisons, optionally using the existing probability weighting. Judge errors and calibration limitations remain.
</Typography>

<Typography variant="body2" gutterBottom>
  Unparseable ternary or judge-comparison responses raise an error instead of being
  treated as unknown or excluded from the denominator. Empty question sets, empty
  answers and incomplete QA batches also fail. Configure prompts to return only
  the requested answer. Report failed evaluations alongside completed scores.
</Typography>

<Typography variant="body2" gutterBottom>
  The comparison text is chosen by the configured prompts; using a reference note
  measures agreement with that note, not necessarily factual consistency with the
  original dialogue. Task-specific validation is required before interpreting
  these scores as clinical correctness, hallucination detection or risk coverage.
</Typography>
